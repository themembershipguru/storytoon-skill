# Writing a story reel people finish

These videos work because they are fables: a tiny world, a clear contrast, a turn, and a rule the viewer can repeat to a friend. The animation is simple on purpose. The script does the work.

## The shape (about 20 scenes, 80 to 100 seconds)

| Beat | Scenes | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 1 | Open a question in the first 2 seconds. Put the same question on screen as the `title`. |
| Setup | 3 to 4 | Two or three characters start equal: same place, same day, same resources. |
| Choices | 6 to 8 | Each one makes a different choice. Show it, do not explain it yet. |
| Result | 3 to 4 | Time passes (`time: "sunset"`, then a new day). The gap appears, then doubles. |
| Turn | 1 to 2 | The loser asks why. The answer is one short sentence. This is the line people quote. |
| Rule | 1 | The teacher and the board: the lesson as a tiny equation or two contrasting lines. |
| Mirror | 1 | Turn it on the viewer: "So what is your axe?" |
| Ask | 1 | One clear ask: follow, or comment a word. Never both. |

A longer video only holds if every scene adds something new: a new fact, a new picture or a raised stake. Give each character two or three scenes of their own (what they did, what it felt like, what it cost) instead of stretching single lines.

## Hooks that work for this format

- A contrast with a missing reason: "Two farmers got the same land. Ten years later, one owned the village."
- A rule that sounds wrong: "The laziest worker in this forest cut the most wood."
- A question the viewer has asked themselves: "Why do some people work less and still get ahead?"

Avoid: greetings, "in this video", the moral in the first line, and anything that needs context to make sense.

## Lines

- Spoken, not written. Short sentences. One idea per line. Read it aloud; if you run out of breath, cut it.
- Concrete nouns and numbers beat adjectives: "five logs" not "very little wood".
- Give characters short names that sound different from each other (Bram and Odo, not Sam and Dan).
- Repeat a phrase on purpose ("He skipped lunch. He skipped water.") for rhythm.
- Keep the gap between cuts under about 5 seconds: split any long line into two scenes.
- End the story before the ask, so the ask does not feel like the punchline.

## Honesty

- A fable is fine as a fable. Do not present it as a true event or a study.
- If the lesson includes a real-world claim (money, health, careers), it needs a source, or it must be framed as an opinion.
- No promises of results ("do this and get rich").
- Take the format from a reference video, never its script, characters or scene list.

## Picking the lesson

One lesson per video, small enough to fit in one sentence, and useful on a normal Tuesday. Good sources: old proverbs and folk tales from any culture (retold in your own words), a principle from the user's own field shown as a village story, or a common mistake turned into two characters who do the opposite thing.
